I looked at how six comparable UK/adjacent art-pop and art-rock artists structure their homepages — Self Esteem, John Grant, Anna Calvi, Gazelle Twin, Marika Hackman, and Villagers — to see how asweoncewere.com's current running order compares.

The short version: none of them lead with biography, none of them gate the page behind a mailing-list form, and tour dates either sit right under the hero or get demoted to a nav link entirely — never buried under editorial content. AWOW's current order puts the mailing list ahead of the hero video and Tour Dates seventh out of eleven sections, which is out of step with the field on both counts.

Top-Level Suggestions
01
Move the mailing list signup below the hero video
This is the biggest outlier against the comparator set. All six treat the newsletter as a footer action, after the visitor has already seen the release and tour info — never before the hero. Right now AWOW asks for an email address before the visitor has even seen the video.
02
Consider pulling Tour Dates higher up the page
Tour Dates currently sit at position 7, after News and newsletter posts. Since the album campaign has real near-term dates, both comparator camps — tour-forward and tour-as-destination — argue for surfacing dates closer to the hero/merch block, or at minimum not burying them under editorial content.
03
Treat the News → Press → Biog stack as a deliberate differentiator, not a default
The editorial back half of the page — News, newsletter posts, Press quotes, full Biog — is more expansive than anything in the comparator set attempts. That may be a genuine point of difference for Catherine, and it lines up with the site's brief to preserve the original structure — but it's worth naming as a conscious choice to keep, rather than something the comparator data itself recommends.

Patterns Worth Naming
01
Nobody leads with, or even really includes, a biography on the homepage
Every comparator assumes the visitor already knows who the artist is, or is happy to find out via Wikipedia or press. Story and biographical text is pushed to a separate About page, if it exists at all, rather than living in the scroll.
02
The mailing list signup is always a floor, never a gate
It sits in the footer after every comparator — once the visitor has already seen the release CTA, tour dates, and merch — never before the hero.
03
Tour dates split into two camps
"Tour-forward" sites (Self Esteem, Gazelle Twin) put dates immediately under the hero. "Tour-as-destination" sites (John Grant, Anna Calvi, Marika Hackman) demote it to a nav link and keep the homepage to a single CTA screen. Nobody buries dates under a News section.
04
Press quotes as a dedicated homepage block are essentially absent
Gazelle Twin's one Mark Kermode quote is tied to a specific current story (a film score), not a curated carousel.
05
A photo gallery as a homepage section doesn't exist on any of these
Social channels do that job instead.
06
Merch/format showcase sitting near the hero is completely standard
Self Esteem, John Grant, Anna Calvi, and Marika Hackman all do a version of this.
Methodology & Limitations
  • Comparator sites were browsed live on 21 July 2026; content changes on their end after this date won't be reflected here.
  • Susanne Sundfør was originally planned as a sixth comparator but her site was returning a blank page at the time of research, so Villagers was substituted.
  • Scale and genre fit were judged qualitatively — no chart-position or streaming-number cutoff was applied.
  • This audit covers content order only — not mobile layouts, load performance, or accessibility.
  • No screenshots of comparator sites are embedded here — click through via the links above to see them live.
