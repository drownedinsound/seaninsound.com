Guests

The musicians, campaigners, researchers and writers who have joined the conversation, each with their own page.

Biggest names

Who has been on

Geoff Barrow

Co-founder of Portishead, whose album Dummy won the 1995 Mercury Prize, and the person behind Invada Records, Beak> and a run of film scores.

Portishead, Dummy (1994)

Tom Gray

Songwriter and keyboardist in Gomez, the Southport band who won the Mercury Prize in 1998, and the person who took the Broken Record streaming campaign to a parliamentary inquiry as chair of the Ivors Academy.

Gomez, Bring It On (1998)

Martyn Ware

Founding member of both The Human League and Heaven 17, he co-wrote Being Boiled and Temptation and helped revive Tina Turner's career with her 1983 cover of Let's Stay Together.

Heaven 17, (We Don't Need This) Fascist Groove Thang (1981)

Lord Kevin Brennan

Welsh Labour peer and MP for Cardiff West from 2001 to 2024, who speaks in Parliament on live music and the Culture, Media and Sport Committee's fan-led review of live and electronic music.

Photo: Richard Townshend, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Sathnam Sanghera

Best-selling British author and journalist whose book Tonight the Music Seems So Loud is about George Michael, fandom and the chart success of Wham!.

Sathnam Sanghera, Tonight the Music Seems So Loud (2026)

Yancey Strickler

Co-founder of Kickstarter and of Metalabel, and a former music critic, who has spent years working on how artists get funded outside the major platforms.

Rou Reynolds

Singer and keyboardist of Enter Shikari, the St Albans band who have built a long career outside the mainstream and who back Music Declares Emergency's Hope Over Fear campaign.

Enter Shikari, Lose Your Self (2026)

Sister Bliss

Keyboardist and producer in Faithless, a dance act with millions of physical sales and a 2025 album, Champion Sound, who spoke on the Youth Music Awards episode.

Faithless, Champion Sound (2025)

Mary Spender

Musician and YouTuber who makes the case that independent artists can now build a career without a label.

Mary Spender, Super. Sexy. Heartbreak. (2024)

Sofia Isella

Young American singer-songwriter whose Reading Festival clip is the most-watched thing on the Drowned in Sound YouTube channel.

Heartworms, Glutton For Punishment (2025)

Paul Smith

Singer of Maxïmo Park, whose debut A Certain Trigger was nominated for the Mercury Prize and went gold in the UK.

Maxïmo Park, A Certain Trigger (2005)

Roddy Woomble

Singer of Idlewild, the Scottish band with ten studio albums, who talked about national identity in music with guitarist Rod Jones.

Idlewild, 100 Broken Windows (2000)

Naomi Pohl

General secretary of the Musicians' Union, the first woman elected to the post, which makes her a leading voice on pay, streaming and AI for working musicians.

Kelly Lee Owens

Welsh electronic musician and producer, and the strongest long-form performer on the Drowned in Sound YouTube channel.

Kelly Lee Owens, Inner Song (2020)

Kat Tenbarge

Journalist who reported on internet culture and online harassment for NBC News and now runs the independent outlet Spitfire News.

Notable organisations

Who we have worked with and heard from

Musicians' Union

UK trade union for musicians, which negotiates on pay and lobbies on streaming and AI.

UK Music

Umbrella body for the UK's commercial music industry, which includes the BPI and PRS, and the source of the annual economic figures that get quoted to government.

Which?

The UK consumer body whose Stop Fleecing Fans campaign pushed for action on ticket resale and touting.

The Big Issue

The weekly street magazine, founded in 1991, whose Venue Watch series follows the fight for grassroots music venues.

Kickstarter

The crowdfunding platform that many independent artists have used to pay for records, co-founded by guest Yancey Strickler.

RSPB

Britain's largest wildlife conservation charity, whose staff joined the EarthSonic live nature walk to show how birdsong and music meet.

ABC News (Australia)

Australia's national public broadcaster news service, whose reporter Julia Macfarlane covered music and war zones on the show.

Warp Records

The Sheffield independent label home to Aphex Twin, Autechre and Boards of Canada, and to kwes., who released Kinds there in 2026.

The Ivors Academy

The professional body for songwriters and composers, which runs the Ivor Novello Awards and campaigns on streaming pay.

Music Declares Emergency

Climate pressure group of artists and industry figures, behind the Hope Over Fear campaign launched with IDLES, Yard Act and Enter Shikari.

Featured Artists Coalition

Non-profit for featured recording artists, which runs the UK Artist Touring Fund and has pressed for consent over AI use of artists' work.

Music Venue Trust

UK charity founded in 2014 to protect grassroots music venues, the small rooms where most artists play their first gigs.

The Bureau of Investigative Journalism

London non-profit newsroom, founded in 2010, whose AI reporter Effie Webb helped trace who was funding an AI rapper.

Novara Media

Independent UK media organisation whose reporter Harriet Williamson co-investigated the AI rapper story with the Bureau.

Youth Music

National charity funding music-making for children and young people, and host of the Youth Music Awards.

Logos identify the organisations and link to them. They do not imply endorsement.
