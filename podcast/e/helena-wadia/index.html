Complicity Is the Opposite of Community & How Media Storm Fights Back | Helena Wadia

9 June 2026 · 65 min

Helena Wadia, co-creator of Media Storm and co-host of the DiS podcast, picks Kae Tempest's People's Faces and talks about who decides which stories matter. It moves from newsrooms that wouldn't run her pitches to why community is the only kind of rest that works.

About this show

Conversations about music, power and what comes next, with the artists and campaigners trying to fix the system. Music is upstream from politics. Hosted by Sean Adams and Helena Wadia.

About the show · New here? Start here · About and the hosts

Key takeaways

  1. Helena and Mathilda Mallinson started Media Storm after finding forty articles about refugees without a single quote from a refugee.
  2. In Kae Tempest's People's Faces, the lyric has grown from 'oppressor and oppressed' to 'oppressor, complicit, and oppressed'.
  3. She argues the left/right divide keeps people from seeing how many values they share.
  4. Resting while reporting on Gaza is hard, and she finds community, like a gig, a choir or a friend you call, is what actually helps.
