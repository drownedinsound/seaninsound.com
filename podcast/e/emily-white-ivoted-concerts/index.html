Show notes

Can a gig change who turns up to vote? In the second episode of How Music Can Save the World, a new podcast from Drowned in Sound, Shain Shapiro talks to Emily White, co-founder of the nonprofit #iVoted Concerts, partner at Collective Entertainment and a fellow at the Johns Hopkins SNF Agora Institute.

Emily explains why she thinks that grounding in how a show really works is the only reason she was able to bring concert promoters on board when she launched #iVoted in 2018... with no money and an intern.

Fans get into an #iVoted show by showing a selfie from outside their polling place, or by checking their voter registration, and the artists are booked from data on who low-propensity voters are actually listening to, town by town.

The conversation gets into why this has to be local, whether it could work with any art form other than music, shows that fans enter for free by learning about clean energy jobs, whether it could create future candidates, and why the work itself keeps Emily hopeful.

Emily is also the author of How to Build a Sustainable Music Career and Collect All Revenue Streams, and of the podcast of the same name. We talk about how the book became a podcast, why season two was recorded in Milwaukee, and where she would take it next.

Afterwards, Sean Adams, who produced the episode, joins Shain to reflect on local listening, free gigs, why free is expensive, Bobi Wine, the Colombian concerts that Shain writes about in the book, and the risks of booking the wrong artist.
