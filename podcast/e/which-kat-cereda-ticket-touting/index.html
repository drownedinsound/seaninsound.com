The UK government promised to end ticket touting. So why hasn't it?

28 April 2026 · 34 min · with Sean Adams and Helena Wadia

Labour promised to end ticket touting in its 2024 manifesto, and by April 2026 there was still no legislation. Sean and Helena talk to Kat Cereda of Which? about their open letter, signed by promoters, managers and even Ticketmaster, asking for a Ticketing Bill in the King's Speech.

About this show

Conversations about music, power and what comes next, with the artists and campaigners trying to fix the system. Music is upstream from politics. Hosted by Sean Adams and Helena Wadia.

About the show · New here? Start here · About and the hosts

Key takeaways

  1. Resellers stand to make an estimated £24 million from fans this summer alone.
  2. A price cap at face value could save fans £112 million a year, which is the government's own figure.
  3. Examples include a £135 Ariana Grande ticket listed at £2,835 and a Raye ticket listed on Viagogo at £140,000.
  4. Speculative selling means touts list tickets they do not yet own.
  5. 91% of more than 8,000 Music Fans Voice respondents agree on action, and Twickets and Dice show a better way.
