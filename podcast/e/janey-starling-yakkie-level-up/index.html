How Do You Turn Rage Into A Strategy? | Janey Starling from Yakkie

Helena and Sean ask Janey Starling of Yakkie and Level Up how anger becomes strategy and how change actually gets made. It covers the Dignity for Dead Women campaign, Bands Boycott Barclays and how the media reports suicide.

Show notes

How do you make anger useful? Janey Starling's answer is that you bottle it, give it a strategy and then find the staying power to see that strategy through (she has a battle map, and one of her favourite books is The Art of War).

In this week's episode, Helena Wadia and Sean Adams sit down with Janey Starling of Yakkie and Level Up to ask a question Helena has been unable to shake since the Cornell case started dominating the news: how does change actually get brought about, and what does a band have to do with it?

Janey is the lead singer of Yakkie, a feminist punk band whose debut album Kill the Cop Inside Your Head came out in February 2026. Before Yakkie she was in Dream Nails, which she left in 2021.

Janey also co-directs Level Up, a feminist community campaigning for gender justice in the UK. Its Dignity for Dead Women campaign, born from Janey's anger in 2017 at newspaper reports of women who had been murdered and then blamed for their own deaths, has since taken media guidelines on reporting fatal domestic abuse into newsrooms, and Janey and Helena have trained journalists together.

The conversation covers why culture changes before the law does, what an all-women mosh pit does for the people in it, how Yakkie build a set that moves from anger to hope, what Janey would do with £500m, and the Bands Boycott Barclays campaign she helped organise. It ends on how the media reports suicide, and what that guidance could teach it about reporting violence against women.
